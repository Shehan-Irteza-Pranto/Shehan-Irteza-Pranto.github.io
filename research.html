Research

My work sits where physiological signals, electronic health records and machine learning meet the bedside. The through-line is building models that clinicians can act on: continuous rather than one-shot, interpretable rather than opaque, and honest about when they should not be trusted.

AI-powered clinical decision support from CRRT machine data in critically ill patients with AKI

PhD thesis · UAB, 2024 – present · with Jin Chen and Javier A. Neyra · NIH/NIDDK R01DK133539 · multi-center CRRTnet registry

Critically ill patients with acute kidney injury (AKI) on continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) face mortality above 50%, and their risk is reassessed only when labs are drawn. Meanwhile the CRRT machine at the bedside streams four circuit pressures (access, filter, effluent, return) every minute, a record of the patient–circuit interaction that today is used only for alarms and then discarded. This project turns that stream, together with the EHR, into a continuously updated, interpretable and personalized risk signal, and delivers it through a clinical decision support (CDS) dashboard.

  • Data processing: align machine records to charted therapy, remove priming, downtime and sensor artifact, and segment the denoised waveforms into temporal fragments.
  • Representation learning: transformer-based encoders, including time-series foundation models such as MOMENT, learn embeddings of minute-level pressure dynamics.
  • Weakly supervised learning: each patient is a bag of fragments with only a patient-level label, so multiple-instance learning links fragment-level patterns to outcomes.
  • Prediction engine: rolling predictions updated as new data arrives: mortality over the next 24–72 hours and RRT-free survival up to 28 days.
  • Interpretability: attention weights flag the fragments that drive each prediction and tie them back to pressure patterns and clinical events, so a nephrologist can see why the risk moved.
  • Next frontiers: extend inputs beyond pressures to pump dynamics and fluid flows, add outcomes such as major adverse kidney events, and deploy the models on the bedside machine for EHR-independent, real-time risk stratification.

PharmaKG Explorer: conversational querying and link prediction on a drug–gene–disease knowledge graph

UAB, 2025 · knowledge-graph embeddings + LLM-to-Cypher interface · sole developer

An end-to-end platform for interactive knowledge-graph embedding aimed at drug-discovery hypothesis generation. Curated PharmAlchemy triplets linking drugs, genes, diseases and side effects are deployed to a Neo4j Aura graph, a Gradio + LangChain interface translates natural-language questions into Cypher with an LLM (Gemma2-9b-It), and knowledge-graph embedding models predict missing links that researchers can test in real time.

  • Graph: 311,496 filtered triplets with node and relation properties, hosted on Neo4j Aura (Appendix B).
  • Conversational querying: LLM prompting converts user questions into optimized Cypher so non-experts can explore the graph (Appendix A).
  • Link prediction: TransE, RotatE and ComplEx compared on Hits@k and mean reciprocal rank; ComplEx performed best for tail prediction. An interactive GUI scores candidate triplets and ranks true tails on the fly (Appendix C).

Collaborative CRRT outcomes research

UAB Division of Nephrology · 2024 – present

As part of the Neyra group I contribute data engineering and modeling to a set of multi-center CRRT studies:

  • 7-day CRRT-free survival: development and external validation of a temporal machine-learning model that predicts liberation from CRRT (oral abstract at ASN Kidney Week 2025; manuscript under review at Intensive Care Medicine).
  • CRRT in patients on ECMO: a multicenter cohort (n = 1,358) showing that CRRT exposure is independently associated with higher in-hospital mortality regardless of ECMO configuration or timing of initiation (Journal of Critical Care, 2027).
  • Regional citrate anticoagulation: a target-trial emulation of its metabolic safety during CRRT (ASN Kidney Week 2025 poster).
  • Data quality assurance for CRRT machine data: a systematic framework for outlier detection, smoothing and validation of machine time series (ATTIS 2025 flash talk and poster).

FRIENDS: a wearable monitor for electronic-cigarette use

CLAWS Lab, The University of Alabama · 2022 – 2024 · advisor Edward Sazonov · NIH/NCI R01CA266170

Vaping happens in discrete puffs, but use is still measured mostly by self-report. FRIENDS (Flexible Robust Instrumentation of ENDS) is a small sensor that clips to the outside of an e-cigarette and detects the near-field electromagnetic signature of atomizer current, timestamping every puff without electrical contact.

  • Hardware: wrote the MSP430 firmware that detects and logs puffs, assembled surface-mount PCBs, and built device prototypes and enclosures.
  • Software: developed the open-source FRIENDS GUI and puff-detection pipeline that turn on-device event logs into puff count, duration, inter-puff interval and daily-use profiles, with artifact rejection and filtering, for tobacco-use researchers.
  • Validation: the ecosystem was benchmarked against a programmable vaping machine (11,479 machine puffs over 26 devices) and against video ground truth in adults vaping ad libitum.

AI Reception: a Bangla-language AI receptionist robot

AIMS Lab, United International University · 2020 – 2022 · lead developer · funded by the ICT Division, Bangladesh · published as "Smart Reception" (EAAI 2024)

Service robots for reception desks exist mostly for English. AI Reception is an end-to-end receptionist framework for Bangla-speaking environments that authenticates a visitor and answers their questions by voice. I built the core recognition modules and integrated them into the kiosk deployed at UIU.

  • Face recognition (ArcFace) for first-step identification.
  • Speaker recognition (MFCC features + GMM) as a second authentication factor.
  • Bangla ASR (DeepSpeech-2 trained on LB-ASRTD) feeding a BERT-based sentence-transformer dialogue module and Bangla text-to-speech.

PVDoctor: smartphone screening and monitoring of Parkinson's disease

AIMS Lab, UIU · 2020 – 2022 · funded by the ICT Division, Bangladesh · Top 10 of 1,051 teams, Mujib100 Idea Contest (IC4IR 2021, submission 391)

Speech disorder and resting tremor are among the earliest and most common symptoms of Parkinson's disease, and most patients in Bangladesh cannot reach a specialist. PVDoctor is a cloud-backed smartphone app that records sustained phonation and upper-limb tremor, and returns a screening result and an MDS-UPDRS severity estimate in real time.

  • Extracted 17 voice features (jitter, shimmer, MDVP measures, NHR, HNR, RPDE, DFA, PPE) and benchmarked classifiers; k-nearest neighbours performed best.
  • Built tremor signal processing from the phone's tri-axis accelerometer and ML models to estimate MDS-UPDRS severity.
  • Real-time pilot with 9 PD patients and 9 healthy participants using the deployed app; the platform spans a mobile app, a web app and a feature-extraction/ML API.

AIMS TALK: intelligent Bangla call-center support with speaker authentication

AIMS Lab, UIU · 2021 · Top 10 of 1,051 teams, Mujib100 Idea Contest

A voice assistant for e-commerce customer support in Bangla. Callers are authenticated by voice (MFCC + GMM), their speech is transcribed by a DeepSpeech-2 Bangla ASR model, queries are matched to personal, credential and generic information databases with a paraphrase sentence transformer, and responses are spoken back with a WaveNet-based TTS. Customer messages are summarized with a Seq2Seq model for operators.
